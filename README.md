## 🧭 HR Analytics Dashboard
# 📘 Overview

- This project analyzes employee attrition trends and key workforce patterns using HR Analytics dataset.
- The dashboard is built entirely in Microsoft Excel,
  using pivot tables, charts, and slicers to identify key factors
  driving employee turnover such as age, department, salary, job satisfaction, work-life balance, commute distance, and overtime.

## 🧰 Tools Used
- Microsoft Excel
- Pivot Tables & Pivot Charts for aggregation and dynamic visuals
- Slicers for interactivity (Gender, Education, Department)
-Formulas: If statement for attrition count
- Dashboard Design using consistent color theme, layout, and dynamic titles

## 🎯 Objective
- To explore employee attrition data, discover key retention issues, and visualize insights that can guide HR strategies for improving satisfaction and reducing turnover.

## 📊 KPIs
- Total Employees
- Total Attrition
- Attrition Rate
- Average Age
- Average Salary
- Average Years at Company

## 🔍 Key Insights
- The company’s overall attrition rate is high (>15%), signaling potential retention challenges.
- Sales and HR (female) employees have the highest attrition, suggesting workload or limited growth opportunities.
- Laboratory Technicians show the highest attrition among job roles.
- Employees aged 18–25 leave most often, likely due to early-career transitions.
- Departments with lower job satisfaction have higher attrition, confirming satisfaction’s strong link to retention.
- Lower-salary employees tend to leave more frequently.
- Employees with Life Sciences and Medical education show higher attrition, likely due to wider job mobility.
- For males, job satisfaction rises with better work-life balance (highest at rating 4).
  For females, satisfaction peaks at rating 2, suggesting other factors like career growth or role engagement may influence satisfaction more than balance alone.
- Attrition rises for employees living 11–27 km away, suggesting commute distance affects turnover.
- 75% of employees who left were doing overtime, highlighting workload concerns.
- Male attrition slightly higher than female — possibly due to lower average pay and longer overtime hours.

## 💡 Business Recommendations
- Reassess workloads and overtime in Sales and HR departments.
- Strengthen engagement programs for young employees (age 18–25).
- Improve compensation or recognition for lower-salary groups.
- Encourage flexible work arrangements to enhance satisfaction.
- Explore targeted retention strategies for Life Science and Medical graduates.
